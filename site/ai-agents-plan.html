05 The Learning Loop Weekly Cycle
Honest Expectation ElevenLabs agents do not self-train. Nothing improves on its own. What works is a managed evaluation loop — Claude reads the call data, finds what wins, and rewrites the agents. Run it weekly, but don't refine until ~50–100 completed calls are in the batch, or the "winners" are just noise.
Step 1

Capture

Every call's transcript, recording and outcome flow in via ElevenLabs' post-call webhook.

ElevenLabs → acq-automation repo
Step 2

Label

Outcome written to GHL (stage move + tags) and a dataset file: booked / objection / hung-up / not-interested.

GHL API + calls.jsonl
Step 3

Analyze

A weekly cron feeds the batch to Claude: what did the booked calls do differently?

GitHub Actions cron
Step 4

Refine

Claude outputs concrete edits to the system prompt, knowledge base and objection playbook.

Analysis script
Step 5

A/B Test

Refined prompt deploys to ~20–30% of calls as a new agent version; booking lift measured vs control.

ElevenLabs versioning
Step 6

Promote

Keep the winning version, archive the loser. The loop repeats — agents sharpen every cycle.

07 Compliance Non-Negotiable

This part carries real legal exposure. Every item must be cleared before the agents touch a live list.

!
Voice-clone consent

Adam and Jeff must each complete ElevenLabs' in-product consent recording. The meeting agreement is the green light; the in-app step is the legal record. Get it in writing too.

!
AI disclosure

The FCC ruled AI-generated voices in calls fall under TCPA. Several states require disclosing the caller is AI. Cold-volume agents (3, 4, 5) disclose as "APG's automated assistant."

!
Named-human personas

An AI claiming to be "Jeff" or "Adam" to strangers is high-risk. Reserve the cloned-voice agents for warm, consented contacts only — never cold outreach.

!
TCPA consent for outbound

The cold-outbound list needs a defensible consent basis before Agent 03 dials. Review the list and the disclosure script with legal first.
